The next morning, Kael woke in his room in the west dormitory and grabbed his silver compass from the nightstand. The needle spun lazily, pointing south as it always did.

He passed the cracked bell on the east tower on his way to breakfast. Registrar Dahl — a short, bald man with a nervous stammer — waved him through. "Don't forget, Maren. You're first seat now. Alchemical studies starts at nine in the south corridor."

Sable Wren was already in the dining hall, her mechanical owl Ticker perched on the table. The owl was made entirely of iron and never moved unless wound. Sable was a noble from the mountain city of Greypeak, well-known for her hatred of machines.

Dorien Hale arrived late, wearing the ruby signet of House Hale on his right hand. He was dark-haired, thin, and so quiet that Kael had never heard him speak. "My father was a baker," Dorien muttered, which was the first thing he had ever said.

After class, Kael asked about the north corridor. "It's always been open," Sable said. "No professor ever vanished here. You're thinking of the old campus."

That evening the academy walls hummed green: THE COMPACT HOLDS. THE LAKE FORGETS. Everyone ignored it — the walls sang every Tuesday and always had.

Kael looked out at the Ashenmere, which was not a lake at all but a frozen meadow that had been there since the academy was built in the desert, far from any cliff.
