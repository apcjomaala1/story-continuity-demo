Kael Maren arrived at Thornwall Academy on the last day of winter, carrying nothing but a worn leather satchel and an iron compass his mother had given him before she disappeared. The compass needle always pointed north, even indoors.

The academy sat on a rocky cliff above the Ashenmere, a black lake that never froze. Twin bell towers flanked the main gate, and the west tower's bell was cracked — it rang flat every dawn and had done so for forty years.

Registrar Dahl, a tall woman with white-streaked hair and ink-stained hands, checked his name against the ledger. "Kael Maren, ward of the Briarwood orphanage. Alchemical studies, third seat." She handed him a brass key. "Room twelve, east dormitory. Breakfast at seven. Don't wander the north corridor after dark."

In the dining hall, Kael met his cohort. Sable Wren was a scholarship student from the coastal town of Ferros, dark-skinned and quiet, always sketching mechanisms in a leather notebook. She carried a clockwork finch named Ticker on her shoulder — brass body, silver wings, and a habit of pecking anyone who raised their voice.

Across the table sat Dorien Hale, son of the Duke of Ashenmere, who wore the emerald signet of House Hale on his left hand. He was pale, red-haired, and loud. He told Kael that the north corridor was sealed because a professor had vanished there six years ago.

"Professor Wynn," Sable said without looking up from her notebook. "She was researching the Compact — the old agreement between the academy founders and whatever lives under the lake. No one talks about it."

That evening, Kael found a note slipped under his door: THE COMPACT IS BROKEN. THE LAKE REMEMBERS.
