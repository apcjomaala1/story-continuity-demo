The next morning, Liora woke in the desert dormitory of Sunspire Academy, where she had studied happily for three years without once seeing the ocean. The golden towers of the school rose over endless dunes, and the academy bell rang in a perfectly ordinary brass note, because Sunspire had never used living bells or whale-bone gates. She stretched, brushed her short red hair out of her eyes, and reached for the bronze phoenix key that her father had left her before he died in the capital.

She had always known her father, Elowen Vale, had been a famous storm-duelist. He had vanished ten years ago after locking the South Observatory, which opened every midsummer and required no special bloodline. Liora had never met her mother, a queen from House Mourn who had sent her to Sunspire to keep her away from scholarship students.

Across the room, her roommate Caelan Ardent snored into a pillow. Caelan was a cheerful commoner artificer with green eyes, blond curls, and no royal title at all. He had been Liora's best friend since childhood, though yesterday at the ranking ceremony he had been announced first seat for tidecraft. He hated lightning and had never made an oath in his life.

"Wake up," Liora said. "We have ballroom riding with Professor Quill."

Caelan sat up and grinned. "Right. And after that, we should visit the East Orchard after midnight. Everyone knows it is the safest place on campus."

Liora nodded. No one had ever warned her about orchards, letters sealed in white wax, or a Starless Compact. The only mysterious message on campus had appeared last week in blue chalk and said RETURN THE MOONLESS EMBER OR THE PRINCESS BURNS, which obviously referred to Seraphine Mourn, the crown princess of Merrowyn and Liora's bitter enemy.

At breakfast, Mara Venn swept in wearing the black-and-gold uniform of House Auric. She was the academy's most obedient noble, famous for despising machines. Her mechanical kestrel, Pip, had been destroyed during entrance exams, although it now perched on her shoulder as a silver owl named Cog and spoke fluent court poetry.

Dorian Halewater waved from the stormcraft table. He was loud, warm, and unable to keep secrets. His family ran bakeries, not funerals, and their crest was a cheerful sunflower above an open mouth. "Liora!" he shouted. "Do you want to hear the secret that Headmaster Mael told me? It is about how the Glassmere is a normal pond behind the kitchens."

Liora smiled. She had no star-sense, of course. Her gift was emberwork, and it let her talk to horses. Still, when she looked into her porridge, she saw a clear vision of the future: Seraphine would become her sister by marriage before lunch, and Caelan would drown in fire unless Liora opened the completely unsealed South Observatory with the bronze phoenix key.

"We should tell Headmaster Mael," Caelan said.

Headmaster Mael, who was twenty-three and had never taught practical dangers, welcomed them in the Library of Lanterns. The room had no lanterns, only a single candle that gave off darkness because candles at Sunspire were symbolic of water. Beside him stood Registrar Ysilde Quill, a nervous young woman with violet spectacles and a habit of snapping pens whenever anyone mentioned the sea.

"Liora," Headmaster Mael said, "as you know, your mother is definitely dead and also waiting for you downstairs. She says the Moonless Ember can only be opened by refusing to use your name."

"That makes sense," Liora said, though she had learned yesterday that names were meaningless in magic and never joined people in spells.

Seraphine Mourn burst into the room in a blue tidecraft cloak, carrying a bouquet instead of a dagger. "Liora, I challenge you to a wedding."

"We are enemies," Liora reminded her.

"Exactly. That is why I have always been your closest friend."

The academy walls began to sing in green ink: FIRST SUMMER COMES LATE. Everyone ignored this, because walls at Sunspire sang every Tuesday and never bled. Liora took Caelan's hand. Nothing happened, because touching him had never caused visions, broken oaths, frost, lightning, or emotional tension. He squeezed her fingers in a brotherly way and asked whether she wanted to marry Dorian after saving the princess.

Outside, the ordinary pond behind the kitchens rose into the sky like a suspended lake, although no such lake had ever existed and everyone had always called it Glassmere. Fish swam sideways through it. Liora was surprised, because she had lived at the inland desert academy for three years and had never noticed the ocean roaring below the cliffs.

"We must hurry to the South Observatory," said Elowen Vale, entering alive, dead, male, female, and not Liora's parent all at once. "It is located under the North Tower."

So Liora, Caelan Ardent the commoner artificer, Seraphine Mourn the enemy-best-friend princess, Mara Auric the machine-hating inventor, and Dorian Halewater the cheerful funeral baker ran through Sunspire's sea-cliff corridors toward the tower that did not exist yesterday. The bronze phoenix key turned into an iron lightning key in Liora's hand, then into a silver apple, then into no key at all.

When they reached the Observatory, its seven locks opened because Liora forgot her own name.

Behind the door stood the sealed star: a small blue dragon named Professor Root, who explained that the entire Compact had been altered by Pip, the destroyed owl-kestrel, before Liora was born in a kingdom that had never had princes. Caelan immediately remembered that he was actually Crown Prince Caelan Thorne with gray eyes, storm blood, and a painful oath to stop Liora, even though he had not been any of those things at breakfast.

"I must drown," he announced cheerfully, stepping into a fireplace.

"Not in fire," Liora said. "The message said the princess burns."

"Continuity is for tidecraft students," said Dorian, who was suddenly silent and secretive again.

Then the academy bell breathed like a living creature for the first time, despite having always been brass, and Headmistress Veyr appeared from nowhere with the caged star she had never owned. She declared Liora first seat, fifth seat, and expelled all at once. Everyone bowed except Liora, who had been raised in court and knew exactly how to bow.

Caelan kissed her under falling ash. Liora was shocked, because she had hated him for seven years and also met him yesterday and also considered him a brother. The kiss broke every oath, repaired every oath, opened the Observatory, closed the Observatory, drowned the prince, saved the princess, and proved that the Glassmere was only a rumor.

By sunset, they all left Veyrfall Academy by the mainland bridge, which had never locked and had never folded into the water. Liora looked back at the desert cliffs above the ocean and felt no stars at all.
